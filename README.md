# Inspirit-AI-Investing-Detecting-Meme-Stock-Surges-from-Social-Media
During my time in the Inspirit AI Scholars Program, my team and I tackled AI + Investing: Detecting Meme-Stock Surges from Social Media, an applied machine learning project guided by researchers from universities like Stanford and MIT. Together, we explored how modern algorithmic trading intersects with retail investor behavior on public digital forums, focusing on how viral online discussion directly influences stock volatility and price surges.

Working in a small, collaborative cohort, we built Natural Language Processing (NLP) models capable of analyzing unstructured social media data—such as Reddit posts and tweets—and converting unstructured crowd sentiment into actionable quantitative signals. We learned to clean raw text, apply sentiment scoring using tools like VADER and TextBlob, and train machine learning models—including Logistic Regression and Random Forests—to predict price spikes and volume surges in retail-favorite assets. Beyond writing code, our team critically evaluated real-world market implications, discussing algorithmic bias, market manipulation risks, and the ethics of sentiment-driven trading.

Collaborating closely with our group and mentor pushed me to apply Python and machine learning to a real-world financial challenge. We concluded the project by presenting a portfolio-ready demonstration of our pipeline and insights, showcasing how computational techniques can uncover trends hidden within millions of daily online conversations.
